## Two honest caveats (also printed on the cards)

1. POST-BIRTH (card 01): the S is seizures in the ACOG standard. The
   Week 7 site notes list fainting in the warning-signs list where ACOG
   has seizures. Both are neurologic red flags in the same family. The
   card shows the ACOG standard and the note on the card says so.
2. HELPERR (card 24): the card shows the full seven-step clinical
   sequence (Help, Evaluate for episiotomy, Legs, Lift, Enter, Remove
   posterior arm, Roll to all-fours). The Week 6 site notes cover Help,
   Legs (McRoberts), Pressure (suprapubic), and Enter (Woods screw); they
   do not mention episiotomy evaluation, posterior-arm removal, or the
   all-fours roll for dystocia. The note on the card says so.

## Notes on specific facts

- Lochia (card 03): rubra is dark red in the first few days, serosa is
  pinkish around days 3 to 10, alba is yellow-white over subsequent weeks.
  Timing varies; the notes say to assess the whole pattern.
- Milk storage (card 15): CDC home guidance for freshly expressed milk,
  as clarified in the Week 9 notes: up to 4 hours at room temperature
  (77 F / 25 C), up to 4 days refrigerated (40 F / 4 C), freezer best at
  about 6 months and up to 12 months acceptable (0 F / -18 C). The notes
  explicitly correct an older slide's 8-day refrigerator limit.
- IUD durations (card 17): Paragard up to 10 years, Mirena and Liletta
  up to 8, Kyleena 5, Skyla 3. Nexplanon up to 5 years per the 2026 FDA
  label, updating the lecture's 3-year duration. All per the Aug 4 notes.
- Gestational age (card 09): 37 early term, 39 full term, 41 late term,
  42 postterm; before 37 is preterm. Size fences: under the 10th
  percentile is SGA, over the 90th is LGA, between is AGA.
- Preeclampsia BP (card 21): preeclampsia at systolic 140 or diastolic
  90 or higher on repeat measures; severe at 160/110, confirm within
  about 15 minutes, treat within 30 to 60 minutes. Preeclampsia does not
  require proteinuria. Severe-feature trio on the card: headache, visual
  change, right-upper-quadrant or epigastric pain; labs platelets under
  100,000, creatinine over 1.1.
- Magnesium (card 20): check respirations, reflexes, and urine output
  before and during the infusion. Toxicity: respirations under 12 per
  minute, urine output under 25 to 30 mL per hour, absent or decreasing
  reflexes. Stop the drip; antidote is calcium gluconate.
- OGTT (card 23): Carpenter-Coustan 100-gram, 3-hour thresholds are
  fasting 95, 1 hour 180, 2 hours 155, 3 hours 140 mg/dL. At or above a
  threshold is abnormal; at least two of the four values are needed for a
  GDM diagnosis. The notes flag a slide error that said two out of three.
  Screen first with the 50-gram, 1-hour challenge at 24 to 28 weeks.
- Amsel (card 18): clinical diagnosis of bacterial vaginosis needs at
  least three of four: thin homogeneous discharge, pH over 4.5, positive
  whiff (amine) test, clue cells.
- Fontanelles (card 12): anterior is diamond-shaped, closes around 18
  months; posterior is triangular, closes around 8 to 12 weeks. Timing
  varies.
- Fetal shunts (card 13): ductus venosus bypasses the liver, foramen
  ovale bypasses the right-to-left heart circuit, ductus arteriosus
  bypasses the lungs, in blood-flow order. Cord rule: 1 vein in
  (oxygen-rich), 2 arteries out (oxygen-poor).
- Lacerations (card 26): 1st degree skin and superficial tissue, 2nd
  perineal muscles, 3rd anal sphincter, 4th rectal mucosa. The degrees
  describe injury depth, not a pain scale.
- Cervical signs (card 28): Goodell is a soft cervix, Chadwick is
  bluish-purple cervix or vaginal tissue from increased blood flow,
  Hegar is a soft lower uterine segment. Probable signs, not proof.
- Pregnancy signs (card 29): presumptive signs are the patient's
  symptoms, probable signs are findings that suggest pregnancy, positive
  signs are direct evidence of an embryo or fetus. Only positive signs
  confirm pregnancy.
- Heat loss (card 10): evaporation from wet skin, convection to cooler
  air, conduction to cold surfaces, radiation to nearby cold objects.
  Cold stress raises oxygen and glucose use and can cause acidosis.
- Virchow (card 04): the Week 7 notes link hypercoagulability, vessel
  injury, and immobility with postpartum clot risk.
- Uterotonics (card 05): avoid methylergonovine with hypertension or
  preeclampsia and check BP first; avoid carboprost in asthma and assess
  breathing first.
